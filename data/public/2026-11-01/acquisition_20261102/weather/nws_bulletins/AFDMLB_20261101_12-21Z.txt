.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 150 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

             -----------Synoptic Overview-----------

Early this morning, we are tracking a shortwave trough extending
from the NE Gulf to the Missouri River Valley. This feature is
on the nose of a subtropical jet segment emanating from the Four
Corners to the Plains. At the surface, a 1035 hPa high-pressure
system is centered near Philadelphia, PA. Brisk easterly flow
continues in the boundary layer over Florida, courtesy of a tight
pressure gradient to the south of the surface anticyclone. Copious
moisture, with PWAT values exceeding 170% of normal, extends from
the southern half of Florida into the northern Gulf.

Over the next 24 hours, the disturbance immediately west of Florida
is forecast to move over the state. As this occurs, Central Florida
will become positioned beneath a region of upper-level divergence,
promoting synoptic-scale lift. This may also allow a diffuse surface
trough to develop along the east coast of Florida. As the axis of
deepest moisture centers itself over the peninsula, moisture 
convergence should become enhanced, particularly along the 
Atlantic coast. A weakly unstable environment is also anticipated,
with MUCAPE values generally around 1250 J/kg or less. The 
ingredients for unsettled weather persist into at least Monday 
afternoon.

As the mid-level disturbance stalls over the area from late Monday
into Tuesday, jet dynamics will become much less favorable for
lift. However, plentiful moisture is forecast to remain in place
through the middle of the week.

Quasi-zonal flow overtakes much of the U.S. later this week, with
a pair of disturbances transiting the subtropical jet. We have been
following these features in the guidance for a potential cold front
on Thursday. Guidance has been trending weaker with the lead 
shortwave, and the 31/12Z ensemble suite continued that approach. 
For Florida, the secondary disturbance now provides the better 
chance for a diffuse cold frontal passage by Friday. This should 
allow some drier air to reach Central Florida next weekend, though
substantial cool air advection is not expected.

Cluster analysis of the global ensembles indicates increasing model
spread next weekend. Trough development is favored over the Southern
Plains by around Sunday, with a reinforcing cold front possibly
arriving by Monday the 9th. This front may have a better chance of
delivering more autumn-like conditions to the area once it passes.

             -------Sensible Weather & Impacts-------

Through Monday...

The main concerns for the next couple of days are flash flood
potential and hazardous coastal conditions across east central
Florida. Here are some of the impacts...

Heavy Rain/Flooding:

We're already seeing some brief downpours early this morning,
with bands of showers near Orlando and along the Treasure Coast. A
moisture discontinuity lies between Daytona Beach and Orlando,
with deeper moisture to its south. As the disturbance over the Gulf
approaches, the environment will become increasingly favorable for
rain bands, some of which are expected to become heavy at times. The
greatest chance of this activity through sunset is generally from
Brevard and Osceola Counties and points southward. By tonight,
deeper moisture should overspread all of Central Florida, leading
to additional development of showers and a few storms throughout
the area. The high potential for numerous showers and locally
heavy rain bands is expected to slowly diminish by Monday afternoon.

High precipitation efficiency within slow-moving rain bands may
support locally intense rainfall rates. QPF clusters from the
synoptic models, along with the latest REFS hi-res suite, suggest
the heaviest rain potential is along the coast from Brevard
County southward, perhaps extending into portions of Osceola
and Okeechobee Counties as well. Antecedent conditions (recent
rainfall) have left us with nearly saturated soils across nearly
all of the Treasure Coast urban corridor. Coincident with this,
we have issued a Flood Watch for Brevard, Indian River, St Lucie,
and Martin Counties from 10 AM today until 3 PM Monday. This is
where the greatest threat for flash flooding currently exists,
but there is still a 5-15% chance of excessive rainfall across
all of east central Florida over the next couple of days.

Admittedly, there is a lot of variance in the guidance, affecting
confidence in exactly where the heaviest amounts will fall. All
told, we still forecast widespread 1-3 inches through Monday night,
with a reasonable worst case (10% chance) of 5-7 inches.

Thanks to WFOs Miami and Jacksonville for collaboration on this
heavy rain and flooding threat.

Coastal Hazards:

Persistent easterly winds gusting 20-30 mph continue to cause
rough surf at all Atlantic beaches of Central Florida. A High Surf 
Advisory remains in effect for 5-7 ft breakers. Due to increased 
vulnerability caused by recent beach erosion, we have extended the 
High Surf Advisory through Monday for similar breaking wave action. 
Combined with the high risk of deadly rip currents, it is critical 
for residents and visitors to stay out of the ocean.

Minor to moderate coastal flooding remains a concern generally near
and south of St Lucie Inlet during the afternoon high tides. If
excessive rainfall occurs during high tide, flooding may be worsened
due to impeded drainage into tidal waterways.

Tuesday - Friday...

Onshore flow lingers on Tuesday, then begins to lessen on
Wednesday. Ample moisture remains in place, but despite a nearby
disturbance, the setup is less favorable for heavy rain. Still,
we will keep a 50-60% chance for showers on Tuesday and a 30-50%
chance on Wednesday. Areas that pick up heavy rain earlier in
the week will still need to be monitored for localized flooding,
mainly on Tuesday.

Statistical guidance has trended toward rain chances of 20% or
less Thursday and Friday. As mentioned before, the initial cold
front looks weaker, and the secondary boundary on Friday is rather
diffuse, limiting convergence. Highs each day should range through
the low to mid 80s with lows in the mid 60s to low 70s.

Next Weekend...

Most likely, Central Florida will be in between weather systems next
weekend. This should leave us with tranquil, pleasant conditions. We
currently have a dry forecast with highs in the low 80s and lows
generally in the 60s. It looks somewhat less humid as well.

.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 311 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Today-Monday... A disturbance aloft in the Gulf will move over the 
Florida peninsula today, eventually stalling over the area late 
Monday and into Tuesday. Locally, this will increase moisture and 
promote a wet and unsettled period. As this disturbance moves over 
the Florida peninsula today, Central Florida will become positioned 
beneath a region of upper-level divergence, promoting synoptic-scale 
lift this afternoon and into this evening. This may also allow a 
diffuse surface trough to develop along the east coast of Florida 
this afternoon. As the axis of deepest moisture centers itself over 
the peninsula, moisture convergence will become enhanced, 
particularly along the Atlantic coast and towards Lake Okeechobee. 
Synoptic-scale lift will become slightly less favorable on Monday as 
the disturbance stalls over the area, however moisture will remain 
plentiful. Forecast PW values increase to 2.0-2.2" this afternoon and 
will generally be 1.8-2.1" on Monday. 

Scatted to numerous showers and an occasional storm are forecast 
this afternoon and evening, with a high (70-80%) chance of rain from 
Orlando southward, and a medium to high (50-70%) chance of rain 
northward. Moderate to high rain chances then continue through the 
tonight and through Monday. There is a high (70-80%)chance for rain 
along the coast, and a medium to high (50-70%) chance for rain 
across the interior on Monday. A Flood Watch is in effect for 
Brevard, Indian River, St. Lucie, Martin, and Okeechobee counties 
through at least 3PM on Monday. This is where the greatest threat 
for flash flooding currently exists. The Weather Prediction Center 
(WPC) has outlooked portions of east central Florida in a Slight 
(Level 2 of 4) Risk and a Marginal (level 1 of 4) Risk for excessive 
rainfall today and Monday. The greatest storm hazard today and 
Monday will be heavy rainfall and the threat of flooding, with 
widespread 1-3 inches through Monday night, with some spots reaching 
5-8 inches. There is a very low chance for rainfall amounts in 
excess of 10 inches, such as those seen the city of Okeechobee this 
afternoon. Over the past 3 to 4 hours, rainfall amounts of 6-7 
inches have fallen in Okeechobee city, with one gauge reaching 8 
inches in Okeechobee city. Other storm hazards will be occasional 
lightning strikes and gusty winds of 40-45 mph. 

Easterly winds will continue to be breezy this afternoon and Monday, 
with winds 10-15 mph with gusts up to 30 mph possible at times, 
especially along the coast. Cloudy conditions and increased rain 
chances will keep afternoon temperatures slightly below seasonal 
values, ranging from the upper 70s and low 80s today and Monday.

Outside of showers and convection, a number of coastal hazards will 
remain present at area beaches. A high risk of life-threatening rip 
currents continues alongside large breaking waves and rough surf. 
Entering the water is not advised! Minor to moderate coastal 
flooding remains a concern generally near and south of St Lucie 
Inlet during the afternoon high tides. If excessive rainfall occurs 
during high tide, flooding may be worsened due to impeded drainage 
into tidal waterways. Thus, a Coastal Flood Advisory will continue 
through Monday. 

Tuesday-Saturday... Upper level low pressure across the Central US 
on Tuesday will move east and northeast into the Midwest by mid next 
week. High pressure off the eastern US coastline will move offshore 
and northeastward out into the western Atlantic by mid week. An 
upper level low pressure is forecast to form near and north of the 
Bahamas by late week. At the surface, a cold front will move across 
the central US into mid week (causing that high pressure to move 
offshore of the eastern US), reaching the Florida panhandle and 
northern Florida by early Thursday, and crossing central Florida 
Thursday into Friday. Locally, onshore flow is forecast to weaken 
through midweek before turning north to northwest by late week. Deep 
moisture will generally persist through Tuesday, continuing moderate 
rain chances. Drier air will then filter across the local area into 
late week, reducing the rain chances to 20% or less late week and 
into the weekend. Temperatures generally in the mid 80s will 
decrease slightly to low to mid 80s by late week.
