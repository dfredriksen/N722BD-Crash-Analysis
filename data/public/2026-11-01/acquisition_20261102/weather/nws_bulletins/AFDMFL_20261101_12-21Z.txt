.SYNOPSIS...
Issued at 226 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Satellite imagery and model analysis data show a shortwave moving
into the eastern Gulf and assisting with the development of deep
convection. Another shortwave can be found in WV imagery over
central AR. Together, these waves are responsible for the dip in
the longwave upper-level trough over the Southeast. A CONUS-scale
surface analysis by the WPC depicts a large ridge centered over
the Mid-Atlantic/Northeast, that sits under a mid/upper ridge axis
that stretches from the Northern Plains, through the Midwest, and
off the Mid-Atlantic coast. A hand analysis of the surface pattern
across the local area reveals a persistent trough along the
eastern FL coastline (along the Gulf Stream). The strong easterly
flow across this trough is resulting in bands of shower activity
developing near, and moving over SE FL.

&&

.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Monday)
Issued at 226 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Through today, the leading shortwave will cross the Peninsula as
the trailing shortwave elongates and moves into the eastern Gulf.
Stable layers in the mid-levels are preventing a robust surface
response to these upper-level shortwaves. However, the affect of
the shortwaves will be in the addition of moisture to the column 
and the contribution to ascent in areas where convection is 
ongoing. Thus, for the surface forcing today we'll rely on low-
level isentropic ascent and mesoscale boundaries. Where these
features develop is uncertain; but while the development pattern 
is similar to recent days, the coverage may be a bit higher, the 
thunderstorm threat will be higher, and the potential for heavy 
rainfall will be higher. Global ensembles, even in the day 1-2
period, have a rather large spread on QPF amounts. This is likely
due to the lack of a clear, synoptic-scale surface forcing
mechanism. However, the majority of the ensemble members would
suggest that 1-2" of rain will be a common amount for locations
across south Florida over the next 48 hours. Common high-end
amounts for localized areas could be on the order of 2-4", with
reasonable worst case scenario that a location or two could get
5-7" of rain over 48 hours. How it's determined how much you get
will be based on whether you're impacted by a stray shower now and
then, a couple back to back heavy showers, or a banding feature
setting up across your specific location. All of these scenarios
are probably, but pinning down the location for each is quite
difficult. The current suite of hi-res data, and trends over the
last several hours, would indicate that Broward county probably
has the greater chance for those high-end amounts today. Hi-res
guidance would also indicate this axis of heavy rain may shift
north overnight tonight, with Palm Beach county having the
greatest chance for heavy rain overnight. How wet Monday will be
is also a source of spread in the ensembles in the near term. Some
of this uncertainty is whether or not a part of the second
shortwave cuts off and meanders over the SE Gulf/Straits, or stays
connected to the mean trough and moves a bit further eastward.
Either way, showers are still expected as we remain in the
persistent low-level easterly flow regime. The question is just
how widespread and how heavy they may be.

Bottom line (at the end), is that the next couple days will be
characterized by showers and isolated storms across South Florida,
with the main threat being periods of heavy rainfall.

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Monday night through Saturday)
Issued at 226 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

A brief period of mid/upper level ridging is expected early to
mid-week, with low-level easterly flow continuing around the
surface ridge. This will likely keep us from completely drying out
through early next week. However, by mid-week, a frontal system
moving through the central CONUS is forecast to start pushing the
low-level ridge northeastward. This will give us a more southerly
component to the low-level winds and end the persistent shower
activity. It'll still be humid, just not as wet. There is a ton of
model divergence on how that central CONUS system evolves, but it
seems like the majority of the solutions bring a pretty weak
frontal boundary through Florida by the end of the week.

.BEACHES...
Issued at 226 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

The onshore flow and elevated winds and seas will keep a High Risk
for rip currents across Atlantic beaches well into this week.
Large breakers are expected through at least today across Broward
and Palm beaches, with a high surf advisory in effect for these
locations. Higher than usual tides will also create elevated water
levels near the coast. Dangerous beach and swim conditions are
expected through mid-week.

&&

.HYDROLOGY...
Issued at 226 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Believe it or not we're nearing the ebb of the astronomical tides
between the full and new moons. While we may briefly (and barely)
drop below moderate flood levels on Monday, probabilities of at
least reaching low-end moderate flood levels appear too high to
let the Coastal Flood Advisory drop. Water levels are forecast to
gradually start climbing again by mid-week as we draw closer and
closer to the New Moon on Nov 9. Hopefully we'll be able to remove
the strong onshore wind component and pull back some of the heavy
rain potential as astronomical tide levels start to rise again.

&&

.SHORT TERM...
(Rest of today through Monday)
Issued at 137 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

A broad mid level disturbance will generally remain parked over 
South Florida as well as the Eastern Gulf heading into tonight 
and most of Monday. At the surface, the remnants of a stalled 
frontal boundary will continue to affect the region during this 
time frame. The latest 18z ACARS data features a long and skinny 
profile throughout the atmospheric column with PWAT values 
hovering close to 2 inches this afternoon. The pressure gradient 
remains also remains tight across South Florida as strong high 
pressure remains centered over the Mid Atlantic region. This is 
keeping a rather strong and gusty easterly wind flow in place 
across the area. The abundance of moisture combined with what is 
left of the weak frontal boundary as well as the mid level 
disturbance will continue to allow for rounds of additional 
showers and thunderstorms to develop tonight into Monday. 

While some of the stronger thunderstorms will contain gusty 
winds, the bigger concern here will be the continued potential for
multiple rounds of heavy downpours will high rainfall rates. 
These rounds of heavy downpours will add to the potential for 
additional flooding concerns across the eastern half of the South 
Florida as well as the Lake Okeechobee region through tonight and
into Monday. Some locations across the east coast metro areas as 
well as the Lake Okeechobee region have already seen 1 to 3 inches
of rainfall with some isolated totals of 4 to 6 inches mainly in 
portions of Glades and Hendry Counties. An additional 1 to 3 
inches will remain possible tonight into Monday across most of the
region, with locally higher amounts of 3 to 5 inches possible 
where the stronger storms set up. The best chances of the higher 
end totals will be located across the east coast metro areas as 
well as the Lake Okeechobee region. 

Low temperatures tonight will generally range from the upper 60s
west of Lake Okeechobee to the lower 70s across the coastal areas.
With plenty of cloud cover and rainfall anticipated for Monday,
high temperatures will remain below normal and will generally 
range from the upper 70s to lower 80s across most areas.
